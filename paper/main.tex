\captionof{figure}{\textbf{A learned lifting policy cuts lift time from 1.00\,s to 0.37\,s, and a physics shield is what keeps it safe.}
(a) The same object lifted by the unshielded residual policy (B2), which slips 21\,mm in the fingers (the rod falls below the
red mark on the palm), and by the shield-trained policy (B5), which does not. (b) Unshielded, the policy fails 12--16\% of lifts
once kinetic friction is lower than static ($r>1$); shielded, it never fails, even beyond the shield's assumed $r_{\max}=1.2$
(40 test objects, 5 seeds, Wilson 95\% intervals). (c) Every learned variant is 2.7$\times$ faster than the friction-model
controller but grips harder. (d) Letting the shield allow some slip saves grip only if friction is assumed not to drop.}\label{fig:teaser}
\end{center}\vspace{0.6em}}]
